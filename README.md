# The Influence of Gender Stereotypes on Quantifiers

Research proposal written for the **Semantics and Cognition** course at the University of Trento.

Vague quantifiers like *many* are interpreted differently by different people, even in the same context. Previous studies have shown these individual differences but not what causes them. This proposal hypothesizes that **stereotypes** are one of the causes: someone who holds a gender stereotype might, for example, need a different proportion of women before agreeing that *"Many of the doctors are female"*.

The proposed experiment:

1. measures each participant's gender stereotypes with established tests (such as the Gender-Career IAT);
2. shows sentences like *"Many of the doctors are female"* next to images of 10–30 people with varying proportions of men and women, and asks whether the sentence is true;
3. estimates each participant's threshold for *many* with Ramotowska's three-parameter logistic model, and checks whether the thresholds depend on, and cluster according to, the stereotypes participants hold.
